    Classic Books Worth Borrowing or Buying

    Print references that compare multiple paradigms side by side.

    • Concepts, Techniques, and Models of Computer Programming by Peter Van Roy and Seif Haridi: builds up declarative, stateful, object-oriented, relational, and concurrent paradigms in Oz.
    • Types and Programming Languages by Benjamin C. Pierce: formal foundations of type systems across functional and object-oriented languages.
    • Seven Languages in Seven Weeks by Bruce A. Tate: a guided comparison of Ruby, Io, Prolog, Scala, Erlang, Clojure, and Haskell.

    How to Use These References

    • Contrast before generalizing. Read one reference from a paradigm you do not use daily (such as Learn Prolog Now! or Learn APL) and note which everyday constructs disappear.
    • Pair reading with code. After reading a guide, trace the matching snippet in Demo Examples and inspect a real codebase in Study Projects.
    • Focus on constraints. A paradigm is defined as much by what it forbids (shared mutable state, side effects, unstructured jumps) as by what it provides.
