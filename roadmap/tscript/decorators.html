    Decorators and Metadata

    Decorators are powerful but easy to misuse. Enterprise teams should evaluate readability, tooling support, and long-term maintenance before adopting them widely.

    Decorator basics

    Decorators attach metadata or behavior to classes, methods, or fields. They reduce repetition in some frameworks, but can hide control flow.

    Good use cases

    • Dependency injection bindings.
    • Validation and serialization metadata.
    • Cross-cutting policies such as audit annotations.

    Risk zones

    1. Implicit behavior that new developers cannot trace.
    2. Framework lock-in around metadata conventions.
    3. Debugging complexity when decorators chain behavior.

    Adoption policy

    Adopt decorators in a controlled module first. Measure readability and debugging cost before expanding usage.

    Practice

    Implement a single validation decorator and compare it with an explicit helper function version.
